api_version 1 (unreleased)

Respec shrines

  • lock_door now fails with a script error on a door without a key, as lock_container does, since only a door with a key has a lock. A door(state=DOOR.locked) or container(state=...) declared locked without a key now fails to load. Give the door or container a key, or declare it closed and veto open with before_open. A door or container left locked when its prototype loses its key comes back closed after a reload (doors) or a restore from the database (doors and containers).
  • ctx.give is renamed ctx.grant_item, since it puts an item in an actor's inventory with no giver and isn't the give command. The spec now says that grant_item, move_item and destroy take a worn item off without asking the remove restriction; remove_worn asks it.
  • teleport now runs before_enter in the destination room (with ctx.direction None) after the enter restriction, and returns the refusal or veto instead of sending it: ctx.teleport returns the message or None, and PluginWorld.teleport returns String?. @goto tells the builder the refusal. Implementers of PluginWorld must change teleport's return type to String?.
  • //lib:trainer.star records the sessions spent per skill; trainer.refund(ctx, actor, skill=None) gives them back.
  • samples/heritage-plugin: heritage.respec_menu, respec_check, respec and starter_skills; created grants class starter skills for re-picked characters too; starter skills are //skills/heritage_skills:*.
  • The heritage sample ships world/ content: lib/respec.star (shrine keepers, the respec command, respec_offer, cooldowns, world-wide defaults) and a //zones/shrine example.
  • respec.star: the reset skill policy (refund through trainer.refund, forget, starter skills), the "respec" standing event, the respec_done hook, and a rebirth altar example.
  • Heritage tutorial 04 walks through a respec shrine; docs/PLUGINS.md lists the respec builtins.
  • New verbs ctx.wear(actor, item, wield=False) and ctx.remove_worn(actor, item), and PluginWorld.wear / removeWorn: put a carried item on, or a worn one off, as the wear, wield and remove commands would, asking the same restrictions and sending nothing. They return the refusal or None. Script item verbs otherwise still skip item restrictions.
  • New restriction queries ctx.may_wear(actor, item, wield=False), may_remove, may_drop, may_give(actor, item, to), may_put(actor, item, container), may_enter(actor, room) and may_learn(actor, skill, proficiency), and PluginWorld.mayWear, mayRemove, mayDrop, mayGive, mayPut, mayEnter and mayLearn: each returns the message the command or verb would refuse with, or None, and changes nothing. wear, remove_worn and set_skill share their checks with their queries.
  • //lib:shop.star: sell asks may_give with the keeper as receiver, so a give restriction refuses a sale and the item stays with the seller.
  • New query ctx.fits(item_or_label, to, count=None) and PluginWorld.fits(item | label, to, count): how many of count would fit in a container (its free capacity and max_weight), at an actor (their carry_limit) or in a room, worked out as put, get and give do, without moving or spawning anything. spawn, give and move_item still skip these limits.
  • The rules ctx gains item_info in carry_limit asked about an item or a label: the prototype's item_info struct, so a limit that varies by item type reads ctx.item_info.tags or .kind whether or not an instance exists. ctx.item is None when fits asks about a label.
  • //lib:shop.star: list runs in the normal lane and restocks for real before it answers, as buy and sell do, instead of projecting the restock; buy sells no more than the buyer can carry ("You can only carry N.", or "You can't carry that." for nothing), and restocking and sell ask ctx.fits about the container. shop.define no longer takes capacity and max_weight: the container's own limits apply. Fix a call that passes them by dropping them, and give a shop that wants a smaller shelf a smaller container.
  • Plugin API: PluginWorld.spawnItem(label, to, count=1) (returns a list: one stack for a stackable label, count items otherwise), moveItem(item, to, count=null), destroy(item, count=null) and mergeStack(item), with the semantics of ctx.spawn (items only), ctx.move_item, ctx.destroy and ctx.merge_stack: they skip item restrictions and the commands' limits, and room placement is at track index 0. There is no give; move or spawn to an actor instead. New reads PluginItem.count, PluginItem.location (a PluginItemLocation: InRoom, Carried, Equipped or InContainer), PluginActor.inventory and PluginActor.equipment. Implementers of PluginWorld, PluginActor and PluginItem (fakes in tests) add the new members.
  • A worn item's wear restriction is asked again, for everything the wearer has on, whenever the item's effective tags, prototype (transform) or item affects change, including an item affect that expires. A refused item goes inactive and one no longer refused works again, as after a choice or trait change.
  • Test harness: w.ctx(self=None) returns a ctx with every verb, as a hook owned by self (an entity or a label; the world's first room by label when omitted) would have it, for setting up state without typing commands or adding fixture commands. It has no other context fields, resolves labels relative to the test's package, runs verbs at once outside any pulse (mob actions queue, so w.run lets them happen) and stays usable for the rest of the test. deny is an error.
  • A function defined in a *_test.star file can no longer be passed to after, start_task (on_done, on_stop) or an affect's on_tick or on_expire: it fails with "defined in a test file", where before the callback was accepted and silently never ran. Define the callback in a content file or a *_testdata.star fixture.

Timed tasks and crafting

  • The warmup slot is kernel: stop/clear cancel an activity in every world, and interrupt_check belongs to the kernel.
  • A mob whose warmup a stun paused is still asked chain_think when the warmup resolves.
  • Timed tasks in the kernel: an activity that waits like a warmup and ends completed, interrupted, cancelled, invalid or gone. New messages task.busy, task.unable, task.interrupted, task.invalid and stop.task.
  • Char.Status gains "task": {"name", "progress"} or null (kernel); casting reports casts only.
  • ctx.start_task(actor, pulses, on_done, name="task", lag=0, interrupt=None, on_stop=None, **args) and ctx.stop_task(actor); the ctx field task in a task's callbacks and in interrupt_check; the actor view fields task and is_busy.
  • The template's interrupt_check reads (ctx.skill or ctx.task).interrupt, so damage can break a timed task.
  • ctx.skill_roll(actor, skill, item=None, target=None, event="craft", adjust=0) rolls a skill outside a cast and returns struct(chance, roll, success, power) (skills); skill_check sees ctx.event, and its trace shows a non-zero adjust.
  • ctx.set_item_name(item, name) sets or clears an item's name override (kernel).
  • Plugin API: PluginWorld.startTask, stopTask, skillRoll and setItemName; PluginActor.task and isBusy; the types PluginTaskInterrupt, PluginTask, PluginTaskCall, PluginTaskStop, PluginTaskEnd and PluginSkillRoll.
  • samples/hunger-plugin gains hunger.dine(actor, amount, pulses), which eats over a timed task.
  • ctx.item_info(label) and PluginWorld.itemInfo(label) read an item prototype's catalog fields without spawning one (kernel).
  • //lib:craft.star: crafting stations (a room, ground item or carried item with a craft_recipes hook), the recipe constructors craft.recipe, need, make, improve, failure and quality, learned recipes (craft.teach, craft.knows), the recipes command and the GMCP packages Craft.Recipes and Craft.Result.
  • craft <recipe> [on <item>] runs a craft as a timed task: it rolls skill_roll when the recipe names a skill, then consumes the inputs and makes or improves an item, losing each input's lose on a failure. Quality tiers rename and enhance the result; the station's craft_check and craft_done hooks adjust, refuse and hear a craft; Craft.Result reports how it ended.
  • The template world gains the //skills/crafts package (smithing, a craft skill that cast_allowed refuses to cast), a smithy east of the tutorial shop with a forge, a whetstone, a repair kit, tempering notes and a smith who teaches smithing, and tutorial chapter 12, "Crafting".

Recognizing skills and trait callbacks

  • damage_types accepts damage_type(name, label=None, adjective=None) beside plain names.
  • skill(unrecognized={...}) gives the lines players who don't recognize a skill see, and the noun the engine's anonymous lines use (ctx.skill.unrecognized).
  • New optional rules export recognizes_skill(ctx) (ctx.source, ctx.cast_target).
  • Breaking: players who don't recognize a skill no longer see its name when it resolves, is resisted, or hits them. They see the skill's unrecognized line or a new cast.anon.* message.
  • The warmup line now reaches only players who can see the caster.
  • Plugins: PluginTrait.on trait callbacks (SKILL_RECOGNIZED) and PluginWorld.skill(label).
  • Plugin trait events DAMAGED, HEALED, KILLED, DIED, LEVEL_CHANGED and ROOM_ENTERED run synchronously for players and mobs.
  • Plugin trait events AFFECT_GAINED and AFFECT_LOST report a bearer's own affects, including those its worn gear gives it.
  • Plugins can declare optional rules exports (addRulesExport, PluginWorld.rules); rules calls gain ctx.base and ctx.args.
  • Template world: recognizes_skill (Intelligence and Wisdom; skills tagged subtle are never recognized), damage type adjectives, and spark carries the blue tag and an unrecognized dict. The tutorial mentor now has mana, so it can cast spark.
  • New sample samples/bluemage-plugin.

Movement pace and pursuit catch-up

  • New optional rules export move_pace(ctx) and modifier point move_pace: hundredths of a pulse per room (default 100). Actor views gain move_pace.
  • Walking pace: each move adds move_pace to a movement debt, and an actor moves only while the debt is below 100. Fast actors make up to four complete moves a pulse.
  • Breaking (timing): a mob's think now runs the action it chooses in the same pulse, so mob actions happen one pulse sooner than before.
  • Chained thinks: the optional rules export chain_think(ctx) lets a mob think again as soon as its action finishes (ctx.behavior, ctx.command, ctx.exit, ctx.chain). By default, pursue, follow, heel and walking home chain, so pursuers catch up at their move_pace.
  • Plugins can adjust chained thinks with addChainThink.

Mob leashes and bounded pursuit

  • pursue takes range (default 10 exits, None for no limit) and searches once per think instead of pathing to every match in the world; it no longer considers dead actors.
  • Mobs have a leash: mob(leash=) (default None), the set_leash / reset_leash verbs, and the leash / leash_overridden view fields.
  • wander, follow and pursue stay within a mob's leash.
  • A leashed mob outside its home ground walks all the way home once nothing outranks it (an engine proposal at priority 20, source leash). It stays put while its follow leader or pursue quarry shares its room inside the leash.
  • wander outside its radius now heads home instead of standing still.
  • New optional rules export leash_return(ctx) decides when a leashed mob heads home (ctx.away, ctx.past_leash).

Target preference, retargeting and combat memory

  • Breaking: aggressive no longer takes via. A mob notices on every channel it senses (its effective senses above 0), so declare a deaf or blind mob through mob(senses=) instead.
  • aggressive(prefer=) and assist(prefer=), with the optional target_order(ctx) rules export that picks among the candidates or returns None for nobody.
  • New behavior retarget(prefer, ...): an engaged mob asks target_order whom to fight and switches to its pick, including an actor not yet in the fight.
  • Combat memory: with module(memory_pulses=) or mob(memory_pulses=), a mob remembers who attacked and damaged it and when it arrived, merged per subject and event and forgotten after the window.
  • Combat memory also records the events a mob perceives, and the entrances, casts, heals, helpful affects and kills it witnesses in its room.
  • Logging in (with any pets) and respawning after death raise a perceived enter in the room, as walking in does, so mobs there witness the arrival and on_perceive sees it.
  • ctx.memories(mob, ...), ctx.remember(mob, subject, event, ...) and ctx.forget(mob, subject=None, event=None).
  • The rules ctx memories(...), so target_order and can_perceive can read what the mob remembers.
  • actor.primary_target: the actor an engaged actor is attacking, or None (feature combat).
  • Plugins: addTargetOrder, PluginWorld.memories, PluginWorld.remember and PluginWorld.forget.
  • The template world keeps combat memory for 5 minutes and ships a sample target_order (weakest, strongest, hated, threat with fading weights, arrival delay and switch hysteresis). A hidden actor moves silently, but one who makes noise can be noticed by sound for a moment.
  • The template world has a pacify spell: it ends a mob's fight with the caster, makes the mob forget the caster with ctx.forget, and keeps it from noticing anyone for a while. A cast is now remembered before its effects and resolve run, so resolve can make a mob forget it.

Skill advancement through use

  • The global on_skill_use hook (feature skills): every cast that resolves reports "fail", "resist" or "success", so a world can advance skills through use.
  • ctx.report_skill_use(actor, skill, target=None, item=None) fires on_skill_use with "report", for skills that only rules read, such as an artificer's.
  • Plugins: PluginWorld.reportSkillUse fires on_skill_use with "report", and onEvent("on_skill_use", ...) delivers skill, outcome, target, item and targets after the script global hooks.
  • The robot world has a sample //lib:skill_gain.star: deterministic advancement through use with a tunable curve, and a self_repair routine learned from a repair chip.

Custom creation steps and base values

  • Plugins can set base stats, base vital maxima and base resists with setBaseStat, setBaseMaxVital and setBaseResist.
  • Creation providers can declare custom steps (PluginCreationStep) shown after the choices, with a per-attempt seed, random streams, shared state and a preview actor, and a prepare callback that runs before a new character enters the world.
  • spawn_player(steps = {...}) runs a world's custom creation steps in tests.
  • starting_stats draws from a per-attempt generator while a character is created through the creation steps.

Feature-sized plugins

  • global_hook("on_death", fn) fires once per death, after the victim's, room's and observers' on_death hooks and before the corpse and XP award, with ctx.self the victim and ctx.actor the killer.

  • ctx.has_feature(name) also reports features provided by plugins, and returns False (instead of raising an error) for a well-formed name that is not a known feature.

    Before:

    ctx.has_feature("hunger")  # raises: unknown feature 'hunger'

    After, with no plugin providing hunger:

    ctx.has_feature("hunger")  # False

  • Plugins can provide features, refuse a world at load, run periodic work and listen to engine events; see docs/PLUGINS.md.

Groups and levels in who and score

  • who shows each player's level (with leveling) and group leader: new entries leveling.who_level and who.group, and new {level} and {group} placeholders in who.player and who.linkdead. Override a fragment to "" in MESSAGES.gups to hide it.
  • score shows score.group while the character is in a group.

Standing and factions

  • The optional standing feature module introduces factions, declared opinions and standing history. Leaving features out in MODULE.gups enables it along with all other features.
  • faction(name, standing = {}, doc = "") declares a content faction //<package>:<name> and its declared opinions of other factions (each in -100..100). A faction with the same label as a mob prototype in the same package configures that prototype's implicit faction.
  • mob(factions = [...]) specifies the factions a mob prototype belongs to; when omitted, it defaults to its own prototype label.
  • module(standing_history_pulses = 14400) sets how long the engine keeps standing history in pulses (at least 1; needs standing).
  • Personal standing records on mobs, faction standing records on the world, history queries, and pruning. A mob's disposition toward an actor (what it thinks of them) defaults to the sum of the declared, faction-to-actor, faction-to-faction, and personal parts, clamped to -100..100.
  • Optional rules exports rules:factions(ctx), rules:disposition(ctx) and rules:standing_change(ctx), with rules_ctx.disposition_parts, rules_ctx.amount, rules_ctx.helped, and rules_ctx.pet.
  • standing_delta(holder, subject, amount) constructs a delta value inside rules.standing_change.
  • Rules ctx query methods factions_of(actor), standing(holder, subject) (a record's amount) and standing_history(holder, subject, since = 0, via = None, event = None).
  • Built-in standing events: mobs remember attackers when standing is on, and the engine automatically raises attack, kill, heal, help, assist, and give events.
  • Script hook context queries ctx.disposition(holder, subject), ctx.standing(holder, subject) (a record's amount, not including declared faction opinions) and ctx.factions_of(actor).
  • Script hook context verbs ctx.standing_history(holder, subject, since = 0, via = None, event = None), ctx.standing_event(affected, actor, event, amount = 0, item = None), ctx.adjust_standing(holder, subject, amount), and ctx.clear_standing(holder, subject = None).
  • Disposition thresholds on behaviors: disposition_below on aggressive and pursue, and disposition_above on assist, guard and follow.
  • Saved standing: faction standing records in the standings database table, personal standing records with history in the mob payload, checkpoint pruning, and dropping of orphaned records on restore and reload.
  • PluginRegistry.addDispositionModifier(doc, adjust) allows plugins to adjust a disposition after the rules export in plugin-id order; PluginRegistry.addFactions(doc, adjust) allows plugins to adjust an actor's faction labels in plugin-id order.
  • PluginWorld exposes disposition(holder, subject), standing(holder, subject), factionsOf(actor), raiseStandingEvent(affected, actor, event, amount, item) and adjustStanding(holder, subject, amount).
  • An actor's cached factions are forgotten when its owner changes, so a factions export may base a pet's factions on its owner. Asking for the factions or the disposition being worked out (ctx.factions_of of the same actor inside the factions export, or a plugin handler asking for the value it adjusts) is an error instead of endless recursion.
  • Faction records about a pet held out of the world while its owner is offline are kept at checkpoints and on restore.
  • The standings table is part of the base database schema; databases created before it need --wipe.
  • The template world demonstrates standing and factions with template-world/lib/factions.star (townsfolk and goblins), template-world/rules/standing.star deltas with a gratitude ceiling and rate caps, disposition thresholds on tutorial mobs, and tutorial 11 on standing and factions.

Pets and owners

  • summon (the effect primitive and the verb) now sets the owner of each mob it spawns to the caster when the caster is an in-world player. summonedBy and the owner are set before the mob's on_spawn runs.
  • Breaking: a mob's owner is its own owner field, no longer its summonedBy or leader. A mob that merely follows a player is no longer that player's pet for xp_share, groups or allies_near.
# Before: a mob following a player counted as that player's pet in xp_share, group_of and allies_near.
# After: only a mob whose owner is set counts.
ctx.set_owner(wolf, player)
  • ctx.set_owner(mob, player) and ctx.clear_owner(mob) set and remove a mob's owner. An owned mob gets the active reason owner:<player_id> while its owner is in the world, leaves its spawn rule, and stops fighting its owner's side. Both return whether anything changed.
  • The actor view has an owner field: the view of the in-world player that owns the actor, or None. Because owner is now an actor view field, it can no longer be a stat or vital name.
  • on_owner_change is a global hook that fires after each change of a mob's owner, with ctx.actor the mob, ctx.target the owner gained or lost and ctx.event "set" or "clear".
  • A mob's owner is saved with it.
  • Every built-in behavior that has a priority takes priority= (wander, aggressive, assist, flee, follow, pursue, scavenge); the old fixed values are now the defaults. Proposals sort by priority, then declaration order.
  • heel(priority=30) follows the mob's owner by the shortest path, however far. defend(group=True, priority=50) attacks whatever the owner, or with group the owner's in-room group members, are fighting (combat feature).
  • A mob with an owner runs an owned behavior list instead of its prototype's behaviors: by default the prototype's flee and sentinel behaviors, then heel(), then defend() with combat. An owned mob's aggressive and assist never pick its owner, the owner's group members, or a pet of any of them.
  • owned_behaviors(ctx) is a new optional rules export that returns the list of behaviors an owned mob runs, with ctx.actor the mob, ctx.target the owner (None while the owner is offline) and ctx.behaviors the prototype's behaviors. Behavior constructors may be called inside it. A rules error keeps the default list, and the list is recomputed after a reload. When defined, the load-time dry run calls it with a throwaway mob and player.
  • PluginRegistry.addOwnedBehaviors(doc, adjust) allows plugins to adjust a mob's owned behavior list after the owned_behaviors rules export, in plugin-id order, receiving and returning behaviors as plain descriptor maps.
  • Breaking: when a mob with an owner becomes engaged with an actor, its owner in the same room is engaged with that actor too. When the owner's engagement with an opponent ends (by fleeing, leaving the room, stop_combat, death or quit), its in-room pets engaged with that opponent are disengaged as well and receive on_disengage with the owner's event.
# Before: ordering or letting a pet fight left its owner out of the fight.
# After: the owner is engaged with whatever its pet fights in the same room,
# and the pet leaves the fight when its owner does.
  • order <pet> <command> queues a command line on one of the player's pets in the room, with the pet paying its own warmup and lag and the player paying none. order pets <command> orders each of the player's pets in the room, in ascending id. Ordered actions are checked again when they run: if the owner is no longer in the room or no longer owns the pet, the action is dropped.
  • before_order is a vetoable hook on Mob (the pet) and Room evaluated before an order is queued, with ctx.actor the owner, ctx.target the pet and ctx.text the command line.
  • PluginRestrictionPoint.ORDER allows plugins to restrict ordering pets.
  • before_summon is a vetoable hook on Room and Mob (observer) evaluated before mobs are summoned by the summon primitive or ctx.summon, with ctx.actor the caster, ctx.label the prototype label and ctx.count the count. A veto stops the summon and ctx.summon returns [].
  • on_summon is an observer hook on Room and Mob that fires after all mobs of a summon have spawned and any on_owner_change has fired, with ctx.actor the caster and ctx.targets the new mobs in spawn order.
  • ctx.label and ctx.count expose the prototype label and count in before_summon; ctx.targets also lists the summoned mobs in on_summon.
  • Pets are stored with their owner across logout and restart: on quit, each pet leaves the world with what it carries and is saved in the player's immediate checkpoint. At login each pet is restored beside the player in ascending id, with its items, active owner: reason and owned behaviors. A held pet whose owner's character or prototype no longer exists is dropped with a log note.
  • The tutorial zone defines a summonable spectral familiar (a pet with default owned behaviors) and the arcane school adds the familiar spell.

Groups and shared experience

  • Each actor holds a transient record of the damage, healing and support other actors do toward fights against it, kept on the victim and never saved. Damage and healing an affect does from its own on_tick or on_expire hook, naming no source, are credited to the actor that applied the affect while it is in the world.
  • heal takes an optional source, the actor credited with the healing; an omitted or None source is the applier of the affect whose hook is running.
  • clear_contributions(victim, contributor=None) empties an actor's contribution record, or removes one contributor's entry. The engine never expires the record itself.
  • xp_share(ctx) splits a kill's experience pool among actors, after xp_for_kill prices it. ctx.contributors are the victim's contributors as structs, and ctx.xp is the pool. It is the one optional rules export: the default keeps today's behavior, paying the killer the whole pool. When defined, the load-time dry run calls it with a pool of 100 and two contributors.
  • Player groups are an in-memory set of players with a leader: group_add(member, to), group_remove(member), group_set_leader(member) and group_disband(member), with the queries group_of(actor) and group_members(actor). A group is session-scoped: a quit, a link-dead expiry, or any removal of a member from the world drops that member, the leader on leaving is replaced by the lowest-id remaining member, and a group left with one member is disbanded.
  • on_group_change is a global hook that fires for each group change, with ctx.event the "join", "leave", "leader" or "disband" and ctx.group the group after that step; group_of returns the same group view. Each change is complete before its events fire, and a hook that disbands the group drops the events still pending for it.
  • ctx.contributors now also lists every in-world member of a contributor's group, even with no contribution and wherever it is, so xp_share can pay an idle member; each carries group and in_room.
  • The Char.Group GMCP state package shows a player's group, or null without one, recomputed when it changes.
  • on_disengage fires on an actor and its room, once for each actor that was engaged and has no opponent left, with ctx.event the "flee", "death" or "stop". A dying actor itself does not get the hook, and the engine clears no ledger: a world calls clear_contributions from it. It fires once the move, quit or death that ended the engagement has finished, so a hook can't re-engage an actor that is half gone, and a quitter does not get it.
  • Nothing engages an actor while its death runs, so an on_death hook can't leave its killer fighting a removed mob: ctx.start_combat with a dying actor is an error naming it.
  • allies_near now also returns members of the actor's group, and their pets, within the band and not fighting it.
  • global_hook also accepts on_disengage, so a privileged package can react whenever any engaged actor loses its last opponent.
  • The template world's xp_share splits a kill's pool by weighted damage, healing and support, pays a pet's share to its owner, pays a grouped member in the room a minimum share, and always sums to exactly the pool, so a solo kill pays as before.
  • The template world's //lib:group.star adds the group, invite, accept, leave, kick, leader, disband and gtell commands (alias gt), and a Group.Chat GMCP event. The arcane school gains the ally spells soothe and ward.
  • Tutorial 10 explains shared experience, the xp_share contract, tuning the constants and clearing contributions on on_disengage.

Percent chances and noticing

  • wander(chance) is a whole percent from 0 to 100, not a 0..1 float; a float is a load error with a hint.
# before
wander(radius=4, chance=0.1)

# after
wander(radius=4, chance=10)
  • loot(chance) is a whole percent from 0 to 100, not a 0..1 float; a float is a load error with a hint.
# before
mob("rat", loot=[loot(":tail", chance=0.25)])

# after
mob("rat", loot=[loot(":tail", chance=25)])
  • proc(chance) and outcome(weight) are whole percents from 0 to 100, not 0..1 floats; a float is a load error with a hint. An outcome table's weights are percents whose total is at most 100, and the rest is "nothing happens".
# before
item("ember_dagger", procs=[proc(on="hit", chance=0.25, effect=damage(...))])
item("wand", procs=[proc(on="hit", outcomes=[outcome(0.4, effect=damage(...))])])

# after
item("ember_dagger", procs=[proc(on="hit", chance=25, effect=damage(...))])
item("wand", procs=[proc(on="hit", outcomes=[outcome(40, effect=damage(...))])])
  • The harness's w.force_rng(fractions=...) is removed; force ints. Every chance now shares one int queue, so a forced int lands on the next roll of any kind (a proc's chance, a loot or wander roll, rng.chance, and so on).
  • aggressive gains chance, via and unless_level_below: it looks for a target with probability chance percent per think, notices a candidate only through the channels in via (a built-in or declared channel), and spares actors below unless_level_below. The new notice event is asked through rules.can_perceive; aggressive now asks can_perceive as the mob, so a mob with senses = {"sight": 0} no longer attacks unless via names a channel it senses.
# before
aggressive(toward="players", unless_level_above=5)

# after
aggressive(toward="players", unless_level_above=5, unless_level_below=1, chance=50, via=["sight", "sound"])
  • The template world's can_perceive hides actors that carry the hidden flag from a notice on sight.

Room enchantments

  • Rooms can carry timed affects (tags, modifiers, hooks).
  • Room affects expire while their room is dormant, and are removed first on wake; the dormant regen catch-up accounts for room affects piece by piece.
  • apply_affect durations are 64-bit; pulse arithmetic saturates.
  • SEMI_PERMANENT: a predeclared constant for a duration that effectively never ends, accepted wherever a timed affect takes a duration, and never scaled by power. An affect that expires at that value reports expires_at and remaining as None; GMCP Char.Affects sends its remaining as null and @stat shows it as permanent.

Rooms, items and skills take an optional tags list of identifiers ([a-z][a-z0-9_]*). Tags are stored sorted and deduplicated on the prototype, read via .tags on their respective views, and survive package reload.

Added traits to mob prototypes

Mob prototypes can declare plugin-defined traits via traits = [...]. The traits are validated against loaded plugins and apply their stat, vital and resist deltas to the spawned mob. Prototype traits follow package reloads and cannot be removed in play.

Actor views show choices, traits, effective maxima and resists

Actor views (ctx.actor, ctx.self, ctx.target, etc.) provide read-only access to character creation choices and traits: - choices: dictionary mapping choice ids to selected option ids. - traits: sorted list of effective trait names. - has_trait(name): checks if the actor carries the given trait. - max_<vital> and resists.<type>: reflect effective values (base plus trait deltas). - base.max_<vital> and base.resists.<type>: reflect the underlying base values without trait deltas.

rules.starting_stats runs after choices and traits

When spawning players or mobs, rules.starting_stats now executes with the actor's choices and traits already set. The return values configure the base stats and vitals, with trait deltas layered on top. A mob's ctx.actor.max_<vital> now includes its prototype traits' deltas, so a ruleset that returns the prototype's maxima should read ctx.actor.base.max_<vital> instead, as the template does:

max = vitals(hp = _or(a.base.max_hp, 10), mana = a.base.max_mana)

Added the world schema: module.stats, module.vitals, module.damage_types, module.unarmed_damage_type, module.channels and module.prompt

MODULE.gups is now required, and declares the names a world's actors are measured by. The engine names no stat, vital or damage type itself. stat(name, label) and vital(name, label) add a label; a plain string is the same with the default label.

    stats = [stat("str", label = "Strength"), stat("dex", label = "Dexterity"), "armor"],
    vitals = [vital("hp", label = "Hit points"), "mana", "move"],

Added vitals(...), stats(...), resists(...) and DAMAGE

Wherever numbers are keyed by schema names, a constructor checks its keywords against the schema, with a suggestion for a misspelling. A plain dict is an error that names the constructor. DAMAGE is a struct of the declared damage types, so DAMAGE.fier fails at that line.

Before:

item("cap", wear = "head", stat_deltas = {"con": 1})

After:

A mob declares the maxima of its vitals, its stats and its resists, all keyed by schema names. They are the input of rules.starting_stats at each spawn.

The rules package is required, with starting_stats and condition

The engine has no built-in ruleset and nothing to fall back to. //rules:combat.star must define every export, and loading it runs a dry run of each against synthetic actors. A rules function that fails at run time gives a neutral result, records a script_error and raises a rules_error alert (this replaces the rules_fallback alert); the rules package is never quarantined.

  • starting_stats(ctx) returns struct(stats = stats(...), max = vitals(...), current = vitals(...)), every field optional.
  • condition(ctx) returns "ok", "incapacitated" or "dead".
  • mitigate, move_cost and regen return vitals(...), encumbrance returns stats(...), and on_player_death may return "vitals": vitals(...).
  • interrupt_check(ctx, amount) gets the total of the damage event's losses.

Before:

def mitigate(ctx, amount, type):
    return max(1, amount - ctx.defender.armor // 2)

def regen(ctx):
    return {"hp": 1, "mana": 1, "move": 2}

After:

def mitigate(ctx, amount, type):
    return vitals(hp = max(1, amount - ctx.defender.armor // 2))

def regen(ctx):
    return vitals(hp = 1, mana = 1, move = 2)

Conditions: incapacitation and death from any vital

An actor's condition is decided by rules.condition after any change to one of its vitals and at every world tick. An incapacitated actor takes no actions or auto-attacks and its tick-lane commands are refused with "You can't do that while incapacitated."; a dead condition kills, in or out of combat.

Added module.features: combat, track, skills and leveling

A world lists the engine features it uses; leaving the line out turns all four on, so existing worlds are unchanged. track needs combat. A feature that is off doesn't exist for the world: its commands give "Huh?" and are left out of help and gups docs --world, and its declarations, declaration arguments, hooks, behaviors, package kinds, effect primitives, modifier points and rules exports are refused (see the next entries). The set is recorded in meta.world_features with the schema: a start with different features refuses to run, and gups serve --wipe is the way forward. gups migrate-check fails on a changed set.

module(
    api_version = 1,
    features = ["combat", "skills"],
)

Features that are off refuse their declarations, hooks, behaviors and rules

With a feature off, a package that uses one of its entries fails to load with "<name> needs the <feature> feature":

  • skills: skill(...), the skills package kind, mob(skills), the hooks before_cast, on_cast_start and on_cast, the modifier point cast_allowed, the effect primitives damage, heal, apply_affect, dispel, stun, summon and sequence.
  • combat: the hooks before_attack and on_combat_tick, the behaviors aggressive, assist and flee, the modifier points to_hit, damage and initiative.
  • track: room(size), exit(entry_index), spawn(at_index), item(range), the effect primitives knockback and pull, and, for a skill, the ranges melee, near and far (a skill may omit range, or use self or room).
  • leveling: mob(level) and aggressive(unless_level_above).

The rules package may not define the exports of a feature that is off, and must define those of one that is on: to_hit, damage, initiative and flee_chance (combat), cast_allowed and interrupt_check (skills), retreat_check and get_lag (track), xp_for_kill and xp_to_level (leveling). on_player_death's xp_loss is an error without leveling.

ctx verbs and view fields of a feature that is off raise "<name> needs the <feature> feature"

ctx.start_combat, ctx.stop_combat, ctx.enemies_near, ctx.allies_near, ctx.act_attack and the fighting method (combat); ctx.act_advance, ctx.act_retreat, a non-zero index of ctx.teleport, ctx.spawn and ctx.move_item, the actor's track_index and band_of, the room's size and track_size, an exit's entry_index, an item's range and an item location's index (track); ctx.act_cast, and an actor's skills, casting and is_casting (skills); an actor's level and xp (leveling). Item quantities, pluralization, and all

  • item(...) takes an optional plural (for example plural = "gold coins"), the display name of a stack of 2 or more. item.plural reads it on the item view, and PluginItem.plural exposes it to plugins.
  • Item commands match a phrase of keywords instead of one word: every word of the phrase must match the start of one of the item's keywords, the words of a keyword, or its plural words, and articles are ignored unless the phrase is only articles, so get gold coin, get coins and get the coin all find a gold coin, and get an still finds an anvil. get 2.coin picks the second match, as the other item commands do.
  • A blank plural fails to load.
  • get <what> [[from] <container>] and drop <what> take an item specifier <what> instead of a single word: a <keyword>, a <ordinal>.<keyword>, a <count> <keyword>, a <count> <ordinal>.<keyword>, all, or all <keyword>. A keyword may be a phrase. The from preposition in get is optional: without it, get <item> <container> takes from the container when the last word names a container that holds a match, and get all <container> loots it; otherwise the whole phrase is a ground item.
  • Bare all in get, drop, give and put skips items tagged no_all (such as //engine:corpse) and keyring-only keys; an explicit keyword (all <keyword>, <count> <keyword>, or a bare <keyword>) ignores both exclusions.
  • A requested count is capped to what the source holds and to what the actor can carry. When the carry limit caps the transfer, the actor is told "You can't carry that much." A get from a container the actor already carries is not capped, since the load does not change.
  • Each source stack runs before_get / before_drop, the move, and on_get / on_drop once; a single command can collect across several stacks, each with its own message. A stack that the before_* hook destroys or moves away is skipped.
  • look, inventory and keyring show a stack of 2 or more as "<count> <plural>" when the prototype declares a plural and the item has no name override, and as "<name> (<count>)" otherwise.
  • The engine's corpse prototype carries the no_all tag, which keeps an item out of bare all in get, drop, give and put.
  • move_item(item, to, index=0, count=None) moves the whole item, or count of a stack, and returns the moved item's view. A moved part is a new item with the stack's attributes, name override, debug flag and decay deadline, and fires no on_spawn. Moving part of an equipped or non-stackable item is an error.
  • give(actor, label_or_item, count=None): count now also bounds how much of an item view is moved (it previously only applied to labels). destroy(item, count=None) removes the whole item, or count of a stack.
  • give <what> [to] <target> and put <what> [in|into] <container> take the same item specifier <what> as get and drop, and the to, in and into prepositions are optional, so give 5 coins bob and put 3 coins chest work. A give is capped to the receiver's carry limit ("<Target> can't carry that much." when capped) and fires on_give once per stack moved; a put is capped to the container's free item capacity and max_weight ("<Container> is full." and "<Item> won't fit in <container>.") and never moves the target container itself. The usage strings become "Usage: give <item|count item|all [item]> [to] <target>" and "Usage: put <item|count item|all [item]> [in|into] <container>".
  • ctx.split is removed; use move_item(..., count=n) to move part of a stack.
# before
part = ctx.split(coins, 2)
ctx.move_item(part, ctx.room)

# after
part = ctx.move_item(coins, ctx.room, index = 0, count = 2)
